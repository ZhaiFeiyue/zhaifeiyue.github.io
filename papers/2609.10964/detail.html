Decoupling Readiness from Release for Tail-Aware Scheduling of Agentic LLM Workflows — L2 #

1. TL;DR #

Agent runtimes submit each LLM turn the instant it is ready; under congestion this commits work irreversibly and destroys reordering optionality. This paper gates turn release with a mean–CVaR tail-risk priority per unit estimated work plus a congestion-adaptive committed-work budget — up to $3.50\times$ lower P95 flow time, no low-load penalty, no engine changes.

2. Q1 / Q2 / Q3 #

Q1 — 痛点 (problem) #

The user-facing metric for an agent is not per-request latency but workflow flow time $T_i = C_i - A_i$: arrival to final-turn completion, spanning release-waiting, engine execution, and tool gaps. Most runtimes (Orca/vLLM lineage) implicitly couple readiness with immediate submission: once a turn's dependency clears, it is released with no scheduling decision. This is nearly harmless at light load, but as the shared engine approaches saturation, extra releases add little effective service yet keep accumulating released-but-unfinished work — the paper's term committed work. Committed turns can no longer be withdrawn or reordered by the workflow runtime, so the scheduler loses the ability to use later-arriving information to advance the most valuable workflow. The result is a sharp rise in tail (P95) flow time under contention.

Objective (exact). Over the flow-time random variable $T$ induced by release policy $\pi$, minimize a mean–CVaR cost

$$J(\pi)=\beta\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[T]+\kappa\,\operatorname{CVaR}_{\alpha,\pi}(T),\qquad \pi^{\star}\in\arg\min_{\pi\in\Pi}J(\pi)$$

with $\beta>0$ weighting mean delay, $\kappa\ge 0$ weighting the upper-$(1-\alpha)$ tail, $\alpha\in(0,1)$. This is an online decision: release time $D_v$ is chosen without knowledge of the future, subject to readiness and sequential dependencies.

Q2 — 方法 (method) #

Two jointly-made decisions at every decision point: which ready turn to release, and whether to release more at all.

  1. Which — a tail-risk priority index $U_v(t)=h_i(t;\widehat{\tau})/\widehat{w}_v$: workflow $i$'s instantaneous holding-cost rate (base $\beta$, plus $\kappa/(1-\alpha)$ once its age crosses the online $\alpha$-quantile threshold) divided by a causal token-equivalent work estimate. Advance the workflow with the most tail-risk pressure per unit inference work.
  2. Whether — a committed-work budget $B(\lambda)$ that shrinks monotonically as an integral-style congestion controller state $\lambda$ rises. Only turns whose release keeps $W(t)+\widehat{w}_v \le B_m$ are eligible.

    3. 核心技术壁垒: the derivation that converts the population-level CVaR objective into a per-workflow, per-instant holding-cost rate via the occupation form of the Rockafellar–Uryasev variational representation (Eq. 7→8), letting a global tail-risk objective be optimized by a purely local, online, non-preemptive ranking rule that needs only each workflow's age and a single online quantile estimate $\widehat{\tau}$. (Expanded in §7.)

    Q3 — 结果 (results) #

    On matched-work closed-loop replay of real SWE-bench / SWE-Gym agent traces across Qwen3-8B/32B and Llama-3.3-70B at five arrival rates: parity at low load (P95 ratio $0.99\times$–$1.00\times$), and under contention up to $3.50\times$ P95 speedup (SWE-bench + Llama-3.3-70B @ 0.9 wf/s: 1986.3 s → 568.0 s, a 71.4% reduction), with per-combination best speedups spanning $2.06\times$–$3.50\times$. Ablation attributes the bulk of the gain to tail-aware ordering, not budget adaptation. Achieved without modifying vLLM's internal scheduler.

    3. 架构 / 方法图 #

    Paper's Figure 1, verbatim (caption: "Load dependent effects of eager release. At low load, immediately released turns receive service quickly and create little committed backlog. Under congestion, continued release causes released but unfinished work to accumulate in the shared LLM engine without a proportional increase in effective service capacity. When another turn later becomes ready or a workflow becomes more urgent to advance, previously committed turns cannot be reordered by the workflow runtime, resulting in lost scheduling optionality.").

    The three panels are the paper's core motivating intuition: (a) at low load the engine drains ready turns fast so holding them back buys nothing; (b) under congestion eager release piles committed work into the engine with no matching throughput gain; (c) that committed backlog is irreversible, so a turn that becomes more urgent later cannot be prioritized — this lost optionality is exactly what the release gate reclaims.

    The scheduler's control flow (Algorithm 1) is a per-decision-time loop over the two levers derived below; because the paper gives it only as pseudocode, the structure is shown as a flowchart:

    flowchart TD A["Decision time t
    R(t) ready, K(t) committed"] --> B["Compute q_v(t), U_v(t)
    for all ready v"] B --> C["Build feasible set F(t):
    W(t)+w_v ≤ B_m"] C --> D{"F(t) empty?"} D -- yes --> E{"K(t) empty?"} E -- yes --> F["Progress safeguard:
    F(t) ← R(t)"] E -- no --> G["break (defer release)"] D -- no --> H{"any feasible v with q_v ≥ H?"} F --> H H -- yes --> I["Select longest-waiting
    starved turn v*"] H -- no --> J["Select v* by U_v(t)
    (lexicographic tie-break)"] I --> K["Release v*: D_v*←t,
    move R→K, W(t) grows"] J --> K K --> C

    The loop re-evaluates feasibility after every commit (because $W(t)$ grows), so a single decision point may release several turns or none; the starvation branch ($q_v \ge H$) overrides the priority ranking to prevent indefinite postponement.

    方程物理意义 (load-bearing). The derivation's keystone is the occupation representation: for any fixed threshold $\tau$, the completion-dependent part of the per-workflow variational loss equals a time integral of an instantaneous rate,

    $$\beta T_{i}+\tfrac{\kappa}{1-\alpha}(T_{i}-\tau)_{+}=\int_{A_{i}}^{C_{i}}\Big[\beta+\tfrac{\kappa}{1-\alpha}\mathbf{1}\{a_{i}(t)>\tau\}\Big]dt$$

    which yields the holding-cost rate $h_i(t;\tau)=\beta+\tfrac{\kappa}{1-\alpha}\mathbf{1}\{a_i(t)>\tau\}$: every unfinished workflow accrues $\beta$, and once its age passes $\tau$ it accrues an additional $\kappa/(1-\alpha)$ — a discrete jump in priority upon entering the tail. Dividing by $\widehat{w}_v$ (Eq. 11) turns this into cost-per-work, mirroring the classic $c\mu$ / pairwise-interchange rule.

    Loss decomposition. $J$ splits into (i) mean flow time $\beta\mathbb{E}[T]$ penalizing everyone equally, and (ii) $\kappa\operatorname{CVaR}_\alpha$ penalizing only the worst $1-\alpha$ mass. The CVaR term is what makes the holding rate state-dependent (the indicator), so the scheduler concentrates service on workflows already in — or about to enter — the tail.

    Proof-sketch skeleton (Eq. 5 → Eq. 8). (1) Write CVaR in Rockafellar–Uryasev variational form $\min_\tau[\tau+\tfrac{1}{1-\alpha}\mathbb{E}(T-\tau)_+]$. (2) Fix $\tau$; drop the constant $\kappa\tau$. (3) Rewrite $(T_i-\tau)_+ = \int_{A_i}^{C_i}\mathbf{1}\{a_i(t)>\tau\}\,dt$ since age exceeds $\tau$ exactly on a sub-interval of the workflow's life. (4) Combine with $\beta T_i=\int_{A_i}^{C_i}\beta\,dt$ to get Eq. 7; differentiate the integrand to read off the instantaneous rate Eq. 8.

    6 minimum checks:

    1. Units. $U_v = h_i/\widehat{w}_v$ = (cost-rate per time) ÷ (token-equivalent work); higher = more urgency per unit engine work. $W(t)$ and $B(\lambda)$ are both in token-equivalent units (§4.2), so the feasibility test $W+\widehat{w}_v\le B_m$ is dimensionally consistent. ✔
    2. Boundary $\kappa=0$. Objective reduces to pure mean flow time; $h_i\equiv\beta$, the indicator vanishes, and $U_v=\beta/\widehat{w}_v$ becomes a static shortest-remaining-work-style rule — sensible degenerate case. ✔
    3. Boundary $\alpha\to 1$. Tail scaling $1/(1-\alpha)\to\infty$: any workflow past threshold dominates priority; matches "P95 = tail" intent ($\alpha=0.95$ used). ✔
    4. Monotonicity of budget. $B(\lambda)=B_{\min}+\tfrac{B_{\max}-B_{\min}}{1+\lambda}$ is strictly decreasing on $[0,\lambda_{\max}]$, so more congestion ⇒ smaller committed-work cap; range $[B(\lambda_{\max}),B_{\max}]$ with $B_{\min}$ the asymptote. ✔
    5. Causality of $\widehat{w}_v$. Eq. 10 uses only prompt length known at readiness plus output estimates from completed turns; never the current turn's realized output — no future leakage, and it is frozen once computed. ✔
    6. Termination / no-deadlock. Algorithm 1 loops while $\mathcal{R}(t)\neq\varnothing$; if $\mathcal{F}(t)=\varnothing$ it breaks (defer) unless $\mathcal{K}(t)=\varnothing$, in which case the progress safeguard admits one turn — so an oversized turn cannot block progress forever, and the loop strictly moves turns $\mathcal{R}\to\mathcal{K}$. ✔

      7. Formal-guarantee gap. The paper offers 无形式化收敛证明 for the closed-loop scheduler: the $c\mu$/pairwise-interchange justification (Eq. 11) holds only for a frozen non-preemptive snapshot, and the online quantile SGD (Eq. 9) and congestion controller (Eq. 14) are heuristic integral updates. A desirable-but-absent result would be a competitive-ratio or regret bound of $J(\pi_{\text{alg}})$ vs $J(\pi^\star)$, and a stability/convergence guarantee for the coupled $(\widehat{\tau},\lambda)$ dynamics.

      5. 实验与数据 #

      Trace characteristics.

      Paper's Table 1, verbatim. Both SWE-bench and SWE-Gym traces have ~16 dependent LLM turns per workflow and heavily prompt-dominated token profiles (7,160/97 and 8,244/93 prompt/completion), with ~1.3–1.9 s mean tool gaps — realistic long-horizon SWE agent workloads with heterogeneous turn sizes, which is what makes release ordering matter.

      Main result — tail latency vs arrival rate.

      Paper's Figure 2 (caption: "Workflow Tail Latency under Increasing Arrival Rates"). Six trace–model panels (2 traces × 3 models), each plotting P95 flow time vs arrival rate for Eager Release vs the proposed method. The signature shape: curves coincide at the lowest rate, then eager release rises sharply while the proposed method stays far flatter. E.g. SWE-bench + Qwen3-32B: eager ~200 s → >2,300 s across 0.1→0.9 wf/s, method stays below 1,000 s. This is the load-bearing evidence for both the parity claim and the up-to-$3.50\times$ headline.

      Component ablation.

      Paper's Figure 3 (caption: "Ablation of Turn Release Scheduling Components"), panels (a) SWE-bench, (b) SWE-Gym. Four configs on Llama-3.3-70B @ 0.9 wf/s: Eager, FIFO+adaptive-budget, tail-aware-ordering+fixed-120k-budget, and Full. Under the same adaptive budget, tail-aware ordering cuts P95 by 59.51% / 63.51% relative to FIFO; under the same ordering, budget adaptation adds only −10.68% / −11.62% over the fixed budget. Full reaches $3.50\times$ / $3.02\times$ over eager. Ordering is the primary driver; the adaptive budget is a smaller, consistent refinement — a notable finding given the budget is prominent in the method title.

      Setup facts. vLLM 0.20.2 shared engine; Qwen3-8B/32B/Llama-3.3-70B on 1/2/4 A100-80GB; 100 tasks per trace via repository-stratified salted-hash sampling; Poisson arrivals (seed 1), five rates; matched-work closed-loop replay differing only in release decisions; each point from one paired run (no variance reported). Hyperparameters: $\alpha=0.95$, $\beta=\kappa=1$, $\gamma=4$, budget init 120k ∈ [30k, 300k], congestion targets (queue-mean 1 s, queue-P95 5 s, waiting-requests 2), $H=180$ s.

      7. 实现 cross-reference #

      [实现未公开] — no code repository is referenced in the paper; the method is described only as Algorithm 1 (§4.3) and evaluated as a release gate layered on unmodified vLLM 0.20.2 (§5.1). The closest open scaffolding it builds on: vLLM as the shared engine (2309.06180, PagedAttention) and mini-swe-agent for trace collection (SWE-agent lineage).

      核心技术壁垒 (dedicated). The single hardest-to-replicate insight is not any one equation but the occupation-form reduction (Eq. 7→8): recognizing that the Rockafellar–Uryasev excess term $(T_i-\tau)_+$ equals the time integral of the tail-region age indicator $\int_{A_i}^{C_i}\mathbf{1}\{a_i(t)>\tau\}\,dt$, which converts a distribution-level CVaR objective into a purely local instantaneous holding-cost rate that a greedy online scheduler can act on using only each workflow's age and a single online quantile estimate $\widehat{\tau}$. Without this reduction one would need the full future flow-time distribution to score a release; with it, the tail-risk objective becomes a per-turn scalar comparable across workflows of different ages, tail states, and sizes. Re-deriving this correctly (including the causal, non-future-leaking work estimate that freezes $\widehat{w}_v$ at readiness) is the crux of reproduction.

      关键实现细节 (easy-to-miss tricks):

      1. The progress safeguard is load-bearing for correctness, not just fairness. When $\mathcal{F}(t)=\varnothing$ and $\mathcal{K}(t)=\varnothing$, Algorithm 1 temporarily admits one ready turn regardless of the budget (lines 6–7). Without it, a single turn whose work estimate exceeds the current budget could block all progress indefinitely while the engine sits empty — an easy-to-miss deadlock the safeguard closes.
      2. Priority evolves even though the work estimate is frozen. $\widehat{w}_v$ is fixed at readiness, but $U_v(t)$ still changes over time because the numerator $h_i(t;\widehat{\tau})$ depends on the aging workflow crossing the moving online threshold $\widehat{\tau}(t)$ (Eq. 9). A naive implementation that caches $U_v$ at readiness would miss the tail-entry jump that produces most of the gain.
